## The classifier rung, and the control it turns on

| arm | what it asks | accuracy | macro F1 |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: |
| `full` | three classes, all four sources, grouped out-of-fold | 0.9944 | 0.9946 |
| `rendered_only` | the same, restricted to rendered pages | 0.9935 | 0.9946 |
| `domain_probe` | rendered vs photographed, same features | 0.9971 | 0.9958 |
| `cross_domain` | trained rendered-only, tested on photographs | 0.8580 | - |

`domain_probe` reaching 0.9971 is why the headline is not quoted on its own: the rendering domain **is** recoverable from these features, so a three-class score could in principle be a renderer detector wearing a label.

`cross_domain` is the arm that settles it. Trained on rendered flowcharts against rendered state machines - domain constant, so it carries nothing - and tested on 704 **photographed** flowcharts it has never seen the domain of, it scores **0.8580**, predicting flowchart 604, state_machine 100. a model that learned the renderer would call these state machines.

**Verdict: the classifier rung is wired** - the cross-domain arm must reach 0.80 on pages whose rendering domain was never in its training set.

## Where the rung stops being trustworthy

`cross_domain` says the rung transfers out of the rendered domain. This asks the mirror question: hold these pages out entirely, then put them through 14.5's own degradations and re-extract the features from the degraded pixels.

| degradation | severity | pages | routed `state_machine` | accuracy |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| blur | 5 | 120 | 107 | 0.8917 |
| rotation | 6.0 | 120 | 94 | 0.7833 |
| lighting | 0.7 | 120 | 120 | 1.0000 |
| occlusion | 0.1 | 120 | 120 | 1.0000 |
| resolution | 0.5 | 120 | 63 | 0.5250 |

**Mean 0.8400, worst 0.5250, and the worst is the number that matters.** Blur, lighting and occlusion cost the rung almost nothing - which is the evidence that it is not keying on how clean synthetic ink is. Resolution does: at half scale it routes barely half these pages, which is below the majority-class bar and means the rung is guessing. So the rung is wired on the cross-domain control, and this is the named limit that travels with it: **the handcrafted rung should not be trusted on a page much under full capture resolution**, and the embedding and CNN rungs below it are what such a page should fall through to.
