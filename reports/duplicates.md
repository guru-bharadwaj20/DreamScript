## Why this matters

A duplicate that lands in both train and test inflates every score silently. The
splits in Phase 1.3.3 therefore keep each duplicate group whole, in one split.

## Threshold sensitivity, and what dHash can and cannot see

| Hamming threshold | groups | images |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 9 | 25 |
| 3 | 11 | 32 |
| 5 | 16 | 50 |
| 8 | 22 | 75 |

**Only one pair is an exact match (threshold 0).** The count then grows smoothly
with the threshold rather than jumping at some natural boundary, and inspection
shows why: at threshold 5 the groups contain *different writers drawing the same
exercise*. That is not duplication, it is the dataset working as intended.

A difference hash downsamples to 8x8 and compares adjacent pixels. For a sparse
line drawing on white paper most of those comparisons are paper-versus-paper, so
the hash mostly encodes gross layout. It reliably catches re-encodes, rescales and
recompressions; it cannot distinguish two people's renderings of one flowchart.

The default is therefore the conservative **threshold 2**. Real
leakage protection comes from the split policy in Phase 1.3.3 - scribe-disjoint,
and duplicate groups kept whole - not from this hash alone.
